How the call gets made

  1. Pick the crew. Take the public Hyperliquid leaderboard, keep wallets with at least $10,000 equity, positive month PnL and month volume no more than 100× equity, then re-rank the best 700 on perp-only month PnL and keep the top 500.
  2. Read their bags. For every wallet, read the validator-dex account and the xyz builder-dex account (metals live there and keep separate margin). Equity is the sum of both.
  3. Weigh each vote. w = ln(1 + month PnL) × min(1, equity / $100k) × min(3, |position| / equity). Bigger winners, bigger accounts and bigger conviction count more, all capped.
  4. Count the money. P(long) = Σ wlong / (Σ wlong + Σ wshort). Wallets with no position in that metal do not vote.
  5. Call it. LONG at 0.60 or above, SHORT at 0.40 or below, NEUTRAL in between. The 95% bootstrap interval resamples wallets 5,000 times; if it straddles 0.5, the call is weak.
  6. Stay humble. This describes positions at one moment. It is not a forecast and not advice. Leaders hedge, copy each other and get liquidated too.

How to read Flows

  1. Opened, added, trimmed, closed. Each sweep compares every wallet's size with the previous sweep and sorts the change into those four buckets, per side. Sizes are valued at the current mark, so price moves alone never count as flow.
  2. Toward long. Net new long dollars minus net new short dollars over the window. Positive means the crew leaned harder long.
  3. Top versus ngmi cohort. The ngmi cohort is the 500 wallets with the biggest month losses, same equity floor and turnover cap. When the two crews move opposite ways, that is the interesting bit.
  4. Whales. A position change of at least $250k between sweeps, a single order of at least $250k, or a worked order (TWAP-style run) of at least $250k.
  5. Liquidations. Hyperliquid's public API has no market-wide liquidation feed. We only see liquidations inside the fills of the 1,000 tracked wallets, so the count is a floor. The open-interest proxy flags steps where OI drops sharply during a big price move; it cannot tell a liquidation from a normal close.
  6. Windows. A timeframe chip goes grey until the sweep history reaches back that far. Positions opened and closed between two sweeps are invisible.
